Topic title: I tested the 500-day halving rule with the entry moved 120 days earlier. It was worse every time.

Post body:

I keep a daily-close archive back to July 2011 and use it to test one fixed
rule: buy 500 days before the halving, sell 500 days after.

The obvious objection is that 500 is an arbitrary round number, so I re-ran
every completed window with the exit fixed at the same 500-days-after date and
the entry moved from 120 days earlier to 120 days later.

Moving the entry 120 days earlier made the exit multiple worse in 3 of 3
windows where the archive covers the earlier date, median -32.5%. Moving it
later was mixed: small gains in some windows, worse in others, and the 2012
window distorts any average because the archive begins exactly at its entry.

The full table (per window, per offset), the drawdown record and the method are
here: https://btc500.net/bitcoin-500-day-cycle

Caveat worth stating in the thread: this is four completed windows of daily
closes, so it is a description of the past, not a forecast, and intraday lows
were deeper than the close data shows.
